# Earning while helping: ways users can make money on Xolace

Written 2026-11-17. These are idea sparks from a founders' discussion, not decisions.
Payout, tax and contest-law points are **unverified**; check them before building.
This note follows on from [`sample-apps-claude.md`](sample-apps-claude.md), whose ideas
it reuses (Kit Commons, Overwhelm decoder, Rehearsal Room, gifted care, Plus moments).

## Why

In conversations with users in Ghana, most stress and depression traces back to
**money**. These users mostly don't use big mental-health apps, and when they use
anything it's ChatGPT or a free chatbot. A product that helps people earn is itself
support that fits the person, and an imported wellness app won't offer it.

Money stress isn't only a Ghana problem. In the US and UK, the cost of living, debt, rent
and student loans keep coming up as top stressors; APA's Stress in America surveys
regularly put money near the top. Xolace's **main market is English-speaking countries
outside Africa**, so every idea here is designed **global first, with Africa ready**:
one system, with the parts that differ by country set per region.

**The rule for every idea:** the user earns, other people are helped, and the platform
grows (a bigger library, more Plus sign-ups, more engagement, reach). Gaming the system
is accepted as a cost to design around, not a reason to drop an idea.

**Precedent:**

| Product | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Opera News Hub | Paid African writers per read |
| Pocket FM | Pays the writers of its serialized stories |
| Audiomack, Boomplay | Grew because African creators could earn on them |
| Spotify, Medium Partner Program, Wattpad, Patreon | Creators abroad already expect to earn |

## 1. Sound Seasons (the lead idea)

The founders' idea: users generate music, the community votes, and the winners go into
the library and get paid.

**Weekly rhythm:**
1. **A theme each week**, like "exam week", "harmattan nights", "after the rain" or
   "Sunday before work".
2. **Submissions run Monday to Thursday.** Users generate a track with Plus or credits,
   add a title and a one-line "made for…", and choose to appear anonymously or under
   their account, the same choice as [Kit Commons](sample-apps-claude.md#kit-commons).
3. **Voting runs Friday to Sunday.** Every user can vote, free users included, because
   voting brings people back every day.
4. **The winner is announced in the Camp channel.** The track goes into the library, and
   the creator gets paid plus an "In the library" achievement.

**Votes that measure help, not popularity:**
- A vote only counts after at least 60 seconds of listening.
- "This helped me", taken from a follow-up answered *lighter* after listening, counts for
  more than a like.
- The Xolace team picks the winner from the top five, checking sound quality, safety and
  rights.

**How creators get paid** (the three options can stack):
- **A one-time fee** when a track enters the library.
- **A monthly creator pool:** a fixed share of Plus revenue, split by each track's
  "helped plays". It keeps paying as long as the track helps people.
- **Remix shares:** when someone remixes a library track, the original creator gets a cut.

**How the platform benefits:**
- **A library nobody else has.** Seasons are regional and language-based: highlife,
  palm-wine guitar, calm afrobeats and Twi lyrics for Ghana; lo-fi, ambient and acoustic
  for English markets; then one per new language. Licensed global libraries don't carry
  this, so local sound becomes the way into each market.
- **A growth loop on WhatsApp Status.** "Vote for my track" posted to Status opens a
  preview link, and voting needs an account, so every entry becomes an advert its
  creator makes for us.
- **More Plus sign-ups.** Generating needs Plus or credits. Give free users one free
  generation a month (Plus moments) so they try it before paying. It also serves as the
  free way to enter that contest law expects.

## 2. Other ways to earn

### Make content Xolace needs

- **Lantern writers.** Users write true stories or advice pieces in their own context,
  such as "how I got through losing my job" or "telling my family I can't send money
  this month". Xolace edits the piece; the writer is paid on acceptance, then per read.
- **Narrators.** People with warm voices record Lantern pieces and Sit scripts in local
  languages and accents. They are paid per accepted recording, under a clear voice-rights
  agreement.
- **Translation bounties.** Paid per accepted segment, checked by a second translator.
  Candidates: prompts, letter prompts, crisis copy and Lantern pieces. This is **how
  Xolace enters new languages**: the community translates and voices it, the way
  Duolingo's early volunteer course-builders grew its catalogue. Twi and Ga are the
  first case; Spanish, Portuguese and French follow the same path.
- **Local try-this ideas.** Users submit Kindling actions that fit their place ("buy
  sobolo and share it with a friend", "walk at Labadi at sunset"). Accepted ideas are
  paid.
- **Kit Commons creator fund.** Kits with a high "helped" count share a monthly pool.
- **Quote poster themes.** A design contest for backgrounds and themes, run like Sound
  Seasons.

### Support people directly

- **Paid Xolacers.** Trained peer listeners earn per session, paid from the Plus pool or
  by users spending credits, with training badges unlocking higher tiers. In Ghana this
  can be real gig income for students and graduates. Abroad, the rules are stricter: the
  US has certified peer specialists, and some states' Medicaid programs pay for peer
  support, which makes this a B2B opening. Paid listeners must never look like
  unlicensed therapy, so partnering with peer-certification programs would help.
- **Check-in buddies.** For users who opt in, a buddy is paid a small amount for
  reliably checking in each day ("did you eat? did you sleep?"). It's cheap, human and
  preventive.
- **Body-doubling hosts.** "Study with me" or "job-hunt with me" sessions, with hosts
  paid per attendee. Body doubling is already popular with ADHD communities online. This
  needs group spaces, which Xolace doesn't have yet.

### Earn by showing up

- **Sparks into rewards.** Sparks earned by showing up convert to rewards from a
  **per-country catalogue**:
  - **Ghana:** MTN, Telecel or AT data and airtime, funded by sponsors.
  - **US and UK:** gift cards, Plus months, sponsor perks (gyms, books), or donating
    sparks to a cause ("spark karma").

  This fits the glossary: sparks reward showing up, never struggle.
- **Sponsored challenges.** A sponsor funds a challenge such as "30 days of sleeping
  before midnight", and people who finish get a reward. Sponsors are employers and
  brands abroad (this ties into employer wellbeing budgets), and telcos and NGOs in Ghana.
- **Referrals that pay.** When an invited friend shows up 7 days in a row, both people
  get a reward from the same catalogue.
- **Paid research.** Users opt into wellbeing studies run by universities (University
  of Ghana, KNUST, or universities abroad) and are paid, under strict consent and
  anonymity. Xolace earns as the platform that runs them. Platforms like Prolific already
  pay study participants. Ethics review differs by country.

## 3. Going after money stress itself

These don't pay users, but they address the cause.

- **Money-stress mode.** Reuses three ideas from the sample-apps report:
  - **The Overwhelm decoder** for loan texts, bills, MoMo debt messages, US medical bills
    and debt-collection letters.
  - **The rehearsal loop** for asking for a raise, talking to a landlord, or saying no to
    family money requests ("black tax").
  - **Payday Calm** check-ins around the end of the month, and around buy-now-pay-later
    due dates.

  The content changes by country; the mechanics stay the same.
- **Money Lantern series.** Practical money pieces from local writers (see Lantern
  writers above).
- **Savings circles with care.** Susu in Ghana, tandas in Latin American communities,
  chit funds in India. A circle of friends saves weekly, and Xolace adds a weekly
  check-in and a gentle note when someone misses a payment. This is **the least global
  idea**: holding money is regulated everywhere (the Bank of Ghana, in Ghana), so it is a
  regional partner feature with a licensed fintech, not something Xolace builds itself.

## 4. What every market needs

- **One payout layer, a different provider per country.** Mobile money in Ghana (Paystack,
  Flutterwave and Hubtel offer MoMo transfers; terms unchecked), and Stripe Connect or
  PayPal abroad. Set a minimum payout and require identity checks above a threshold
  (Ghana Card in Ghana).
- **Tax reporting.** Paying users brings reporting duties, such as US 1099 forms and the
  EU's DAC7 seller reporting. Stripe Connect handles much of this.
- **One ledger, separate balances.** Cash earnings and sparks sit in the same ledger as
  different balances. Convex Credits' namespaces already handle this
  (`sample apps/convex-credits-main`).
- **Rights to generated music.**
  - The contest terms grant Xolace a licence to winning tracks.
  - Check that the music generator's terms allow commercial use and transfer of rights;
    Suno, ElevenLabs and Stable Audio each differ.
  - Purely AI-made audio may not be protected by copyright, so lyrics the user writes
    strengthen ownership.
- **Contest law.**
  - Requiring Plus to enter, with a cash prize, may count as a regulated promotional
    competition. Ghana's Gaming Commission rules need checking, the US and UK generally
    expect a free way to enter, and some US states restrict paid-entry contests.
  - Voting on merit plus the monthly free generation usually keeps a contest outside
    gambling rules. Keep the free path in every market.
- **App Store.** Paying creators for content is allowed. Paying for ratings or reviews
  is not, so never reward app-store reviews.
- **One principle from the glossary.** Pay for helping, making and showing up, never for
  disclosing struggle. Letters and vents are never paid.

## 5. How each idea carries over to the main markets

| Idea | Outside Africa | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Sound Seasons | Works as is | Seasons per region and language |
| Lantern writers | Works as is | Recognition may matter more than small payments |
| Narrators and translation bounties | Works, and becomes how Xolace adds languages | None |
| Kit Commons fund | Works as is | None |
| Paid Xolacers | Works, with stricter rules | Framing, training, certification partners |
| Check-in buddies, hosts | Works | None |
| Sparks into rewards | Works with a different catalogue | Data and airtime in Ghana; gift cards, perks and donations abroad |
| Sponsored challenges | Works, as a B2B employer product | Sponsor types |
| Referrals | Works | Reward catalogue |
| Paid research | Works | Ethics review per country |
| Money-stress mode | Strong everywhere | Content per country |
| Savings circles | Weak | Regional, partner-only |

## 6. Ranking and rollout

**Ranking:**
1. **Sound Seasons with the creator pool.** It grows the library, Plus sign-ups and
   reach at once.
2. **Sparks into rewards,** funded by sponsors.
3. **Paid Xolacers and check-in buddies.**
4. **Writers, narrators and translators.**
5. **Kit Commons creator fund.**

Money-stress mode runs alongside these as the piece that goes after the cause.

**Rollout:**
1. **Global from the start, Ghana included:**
   - Sound Seasons
   - Kit Commons fund
   - Lantern writers
   - money-stress mode
   - paid research
2. **The same feature, set up per region:**
   - reward catalogue
   - payout provider
   - sponsors
   - seasons and languages
3. **Regional partner features:**
   - savings circles
   - telco data bundles
   - WhatsApp-first sign-up

Ghana works as a proving ground. A feature that helps where money stress is sharpest and
big apps haven't reached is likely to hold up in bigger markets too.

## Open questions

1. **How large a share of Plus revenue goes to the creator pool?** Also, is the pool
   fixed or tied to growth?
2. **Do Xolacers become paid,** given the regulatory framing abroad, or stay volunteer
   with paid tiers only in some markets?
3. **Which sponsors come first:** telcos (Ghana), employers (abroad), or NGOs?
4. **Who owns the creator relationship legally:** Xolace Inc directly, or a payout
   partner as merchant of record?
