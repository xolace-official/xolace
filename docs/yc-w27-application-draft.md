## Idea

**Why did you pick this idea to work on? Do you have domain expertise in this area? How do you know people need what you're making?**

None of us comes from mental health as a profession. We come from living it: a cousin who went quiet for years because she couldn't name what was wrong and didn't think anyone would understand.

Her silence isn't rare in Ghana, it's the default. You're taught to carry it, pray on it, or say you're fine. Since launch, people have opened Xolace and written through it anyway, against a culture that tells them not to, with no marketing from us. Some now pay for it. That's how we know the need is real.

**Who are your competitors? What do you understand about your business that they don't?**

ChatGPT, journaling apps (Rosebud, How We Feel), Calm and Headspace, Wysa and Woebot, and therapy.

The whole industry treats mental health like an emergency room: people arrive already hurt, and the job is to see them faster. More therapists, quicker triage, a chatbot for the waitlist. We start at the other end. Most of what reaches that room began as something small that nobody named. Catch it there, daily, before it has a name, and the person never needs the room. Nobody builds for that moment, because the person in it doesn't think they have a problem yet. Skincare doesn't wait for a rash. Neither should this.

**How do or will you make money? How much could you make?**

Freemium. The mirror is free forever. Xolace+ ($59.99/yr or $9.99/mo) unlocks routed support, full audio, message drafting, and full history. ~$100 MRR today.

Later: universities and employers buying Xolace for their people, and a route to licensed care as its own revenue line.

Size: mental-health app spending is over $6B a year. If 1% of the roughly 500M English-speaking smartphone users pay us $60 a year, that's $300M ARR from consumers alone.

**If you had any other ideas you considered applying with, please list them.**

Xolace for Work. Xolace plugged into Slack, Teams, and the calendar. It notices when someone's pattern shifts (reply cadence, meeting load, late-night activity), offers them a two-minute support step in the tool they're already in, and gives the employer an aggregated, anonymised read on how teams are doing. No individual is ever exposed. Employee assistance programmes get under 5% engagement because they wait for a breaking point; this catches it earlier. Mid-size companies with high turnover first.

Xolace Health. Telehealth where an AI takes the symptoms first, scores urgency, and hands a structured case to a licensed doctor or pharmacist for a live consult. If a prescription is issued, the same flow routes it to a licensed pharmacy for delivery. "I feel unwell" to "medication in hand" without a clinic visit.

VisualX. In testing, some users couldn't find words for a feeling but could describe an image. VisualX lets them build that image with AI image generation, editing it until it matches, so the feeling has a shape they can look at and talk about.

### What is your company going to make — v1

**What is your company going to make? Please describe your product and what it does or will do.**

Xolace is a mobile app that helps people name what they're feeling before it grows into something that needs a therapist.

You write or say what's on your mind. Xolace writes back a short "mirror": what it hears underneath what you said. If it's off, you tap "Not quite" and it tries again. It remembers you across sessions, so the mirror gets sharper the longer you use it. When a session shows you could use support, Xolace picks from a set of tools we built and reviewed ourselves: a breathing exercise, audio, a short read, a chat with a trained peer counsellor, or a draft message to someone in your life. The AI chooses support from that shelf; it never invents it.

It's live on iOS and Android. About 350 people have completed about 1,000 reflections with no paid acquisition, and the first of them are paying.

Models: Claude Sonnet 5.5 writes the mirror. Claude Haiku 5.5 classifies the input, picks support, and handles every other small call. OpenAI for moderation and embeddings. The safety screen is a rules engine, not a model, so we can test it.

AI coding tools: Claude Code and Cursor. Most features go from idea to a build on the phone in a day.

### Competitors — v1

**Who are your competitors? What do you understand about your business that they don't?**

The real competitor is ChatGPT at 1am. It understands what you write, but its support is whatever it makes up, and it leads nowhere. Journaling apps (Rosebud, How We Feel) own the daily habit but stop at the feeling. Calm and Headspace have good support but no understanding; you pick a session blind. Wysa and Woebot are built around exercises and ask you to see yourself as a patient. Therapy is the real thing, and it's scarce, expensive, and not there in the moment.

What we understand: understanding and support have to be one product, used daily, and the support has to come from a fixed shelf we built and reviewed, not from the model. An AI that invents support for someone in distress will eventually be wrong in a way that matters. An AI that chooses from a vetted shelf can be tested. That constraint is the product.

So the habit loop has to be right before we spend on growth, and that's what the last three months went into: the paid plan, follow-up check-ins, Insights, and voice.
