# Stress: what helps

## What can you do when stress starts building?

### Pause before pushing harder

When you're overwhelmed, doing more isn't always the answer.

- Take a short break.
- Step away from your screen.
- Take a walk.
- Drink some water.
- Give your mind a moment to reset.

### Separate urgent from important

- Not everything needs to be solved today.
- Write down what needs immediate attention and what can wait.
- Reducing a long list into one or two manageable tasks can make a situation feel less overwhelming.

### Talk to someone

- Stress can become heavier when you carry it privately.
- Speak with someone you trust.
- You don't necessarily need them to solve your problem.
- Sometimes you simply need someone to listen.

### Look after your physical needs

Regular movement, adequate sleep, nutritious meals and time for recovery can support your ability to manage stress.

These things don't eliminate life's problems, but they can help you approach them with more energy.

### Watch how you're coping

When stressed, people sometimes turn toward alcohol, smoking, drugs or other habits because they provide temporary relief.

But these strategies can create additional problems and may make mental wellbeing worse over time.

## When is stress becoming too much?

Stress deserves attention when it begins interfering with your everyday life.

For example:

- You constantly feel overwhelmed.
- You can't concentrate.
- Your sleep is significantly affected.
- You are withdrawing from people.
- You are struggling to perform at work or school.
- Your physical symptoms are persistent.
- You feel unable to cope.
- Your usual coping strategies aren't helping.

At that point, talking to a qualified professional may be useful.

## Stress isn't a personal failure

- Being stressed doesn't mean you are weak.
- It doesn't necessarily mean you're incapable of handling life.
- Sometimes it means you've been carrying too much for too long.

Instead of asking:

*"Why can't I handle this?"*

you might ask:

*"What is putting me under pressure, and what support do I need?"*

That question can lead to a more useful conversation.

## Give yourself room to breathe

- You cannot always remove every source of stress from your life.
- But you can learn to recognize the signals.
- You can pause.
- You can ask for support.
- You can set boundaries.
- You can break large problems into smaller ones.
- And when stress becomes difficult to manage, you can seek professional help.

**You don't have to wait until you're completely exhausted before you take yourself seriously.**

If you want to understand what is going on first, our entry "Stress: what it is" covers that.
