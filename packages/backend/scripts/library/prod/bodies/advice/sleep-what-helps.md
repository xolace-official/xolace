# Sleep: what helps

## Create a routine

Try to maintain reasonably consistent sleeping and waking times. Your body benefits from predictable rhythms.

## Give your mind time to slow down

Going directly from a stressful activity into bed can make it difficult to switch off.

Create a short wind-down period.

- Read.
- Listen to something calming.
- Take a warm shower.
- Write down tomorrow's tasks.

Do something that tells your brain:

*"The day is ending."*

## Be mindful of screens

Phones and computers can keep your mind engaged when you are trying to relax.

If possible, give yourself some screen-free time before bed.

You don't need to make the change perfectly. Even gradually reducing late-night scrolling can be useful.

## Pay attention to caffeine

Caffeine affects people differently.

If you notice that coffee, energy drinks or other caffeinated beverages make it harder for you to sleep, consider reducing your intake, particularly later in the day.

## Make your sleeping environment comfortable

Where possible, aim for a space that is dark, quiet and comfortable.

Even small changes can make a difference.

## Don't panic when you can't sleep

Sometimes worrying about not sleeping becomes part of the problem.

- You look at the clock.
- You calculate how many hours remain.
- You become frustrated.
- You start worrying about how tired you'll be tomorrow.
- And suddenly you're even more awake.

If you're unable to sleep, try to remain calm rather than turning the situation into another source of anxiety.

## When should you seek help?

Consider speaking with a healthcare professional if sleep problems:

- Continue for a prolonged period
- Are seriously affecting your daily life
- Are affecting your ability to work or study
- Are accompanied by significant anxiety or depression
- Leave you constantly exhausted
- Are causing you to rely heavily on substances or medication to sleep

Persistent sleep difficulties can sometimes have an underlying physical or mental-health cause, so professional assessment can be valuable.

## Sleep isn't laziness

In a culture where being busy is sometimes treated like an achievement, sleep can feel like something we should sacrifice.

But rest isn't wasted time.

- Your body needs it.
- Your brain needs it.
- Your emotional wellbeing needs it.

You don't have to earn the right to rest by exhausting yourself first.

Sometimes taking care of yourself means recognizing that you have done enough for today.

**Close the day. Let yourself rest. Tomorrow can wait.**
