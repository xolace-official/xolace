# Loneliness: what helps

Loneliness can make reaching out feel harder than it should. These are small, low-pressure ways to start rebuilding connection.

## Send the message

You don't need a dramatic opening. Try: "Hey, it's been a while. How are you doing?"

Sometimes a small message can reopen a relationship.

## Spend time where connection can happen naturally

Consider joining a club, community, class, volunteer activity, sports group or interest-based community.

The goal doesn't have to be finding a best friend immediately. Start by simply being around people who share something with you.

## Do something you enjoy

Loneliness can cause you to stop doing things that make you feel alive. Try returning to an activity you once enjoyed:

- Music
- Reading
- Walking
- Sport
- Cooking
- Creative work
- Learning something new

These activities don't replace human connection, but they can support your wellbeing while you work on rebuilding it.

## Say how you actually feel

If you have someone you trust, consider telling them that you're feeling lonely. You might be surprised to discover that they have experienced something similar.

## Stop measuring your life against someone else's

Comparison can make loneliness worse. Someone else's social life is not a measurement of your worth.

## When loneliness needs more support

Consider speaking with a mental-health professional if loneliness is becoming persistent and is accompanied by:

- Depression
- Severe anxiety
- Significant changes in sleep
- Loss of interest in normal activities
- Difficulty functioning at school or work
- Feelings of hopelessness
- Thoughts of harming yourself

Loneliness can sometimes be connected to deeper emotional difficulties that deserve professional attention.

## Connection doesn't have to be perfect

You don't need dozens of friends. You don't need to be constantly surrounded by people. Sometimes one honest conversation can matter more than a hundred casual interactions.

The goal isn't to make your life look socially full. It's to find spaces where you can feel seen, heard and accepted.

And if you are feeling lonely right now, remember: feeling alone is an experience. It is not a definition of who you are.

If you want to understand what is going on first, our entry "Loneliness: what it is" covers that.
