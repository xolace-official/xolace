# Depression: what helps

Depression is treatable, and different people may benefit from different approaches.

## Start by telling someone

You don't have to explain everything perfectly.

A trusted friend, family member, counsellor, doctor or mental-health professional can be a starting point.

Even saying, **"I've not been feeling like myself lately"** can open the conversation.

## Keep some connection

Depression often encourages withdrawal.

If possible, maintain small connections with people you trust. A short conversation, a walk with someone, or simply sitting with another person can be meaningful.

## Keep basic routines

When motivation disappears, basic routines can provide some structure.

Try to maintain regular sleep and meals, get some movement into your day, and continue with manageable activities where possible.

You don't need to do everything.

Start small.

## Don't expect yourself to recover overnight

Depression can make recovery feel impossibly far away.

- Progress may happen gradually.
- A better day matters.
- Getting out of bed matters.
- Talking to someone matters.
- Going for a short walk matters.
- Small steps are still movement.

## Professional treatment

Professional treatment may include psychological therapies, medication, or a combination depending on the person's circumstances and the severity of their symptoms.

Talking therapies can help people understand patterns in their thoughts, emotions and behaviour and develop strategies for coping.

Medication may also be recommended in some circumstances by an appropriate healthcare professional.

Treatment is not one-size-fits-all. Finding an approach that works can sometimes take time.

## When should you seek help?

Consider speaking with a healthcare or mental-health professional if low mood:

- Lasts for two weeks or longer
- Is becoming difficult to manage
- Interferes with school, work or relationships
- Changes your sleep or appetite significantly
- Causes you to lose interest in most things
- Makes everyday responsibilities difficult
- Leaves you feeling hopeless or worthless

If you are experiencing thoughts of suicide or feel that you may hurt yourself, seek urgent professional or emergency support rather than dealing with those feelings alone.

## You don't have to explain your pain perfectly

Depression can make people feel like they are a burden.

It can make asking for help feel impossible.

But needing support does not make you weak.

Sometimes the most important first step is simply letting another person know that you're struggling.

You don't have to have all the answers before you ask for help.

**You only need to take the next step.**

If you want to understand what is going on first, our entry "Depression: what it is" covers that.
