# Anxiety: what it is and why it happens

Anxiety is something almost everyone experiences at some point. It can show up before an important exam, during a difficult conversation, when money is tight, or when you are uncertain about what comes next.

A certain amount of anxiety is part of being human. It can make us more alert, encourage us to prepare, and help us respond to situations we perceive as threatening. The challenge begins when anxiety becomes persistent, difficult to control, or starts interfering with everyday life.

At that point, it may be more than ordinary worry.

## What exactly is anxiety?

Anxiety is a combination of emotional, physical and mental responses that can occur when we anticipate a threat, uncertainty or difficult situation.

Sometimes there is an obvious reason for it. Other times, you may feel anxious without being able to clearly explain why.

You might notice your thoughts racing while your body feels tense. Your heart may beat faster, your stomach may feel unsettled, or you may find yourself constantly expecting something to go wrong.

Anxiety does not look the same for everyone.

For one person, it may feel like constant overthinking. For another, it may appear as physical tension, difficulty sleeping, avoidance, panic, or trouble concentrating.

## When does anxiety become a problem?

Feeling anxious occasionally does not automatically mean that you have an anxiety disorder.

Anxiety becomes more concerning when it is persistent, overwhelming, difficult to manage, or begins affecting important parts of your life.

For example, you might start avoiding social situations, struggle to concentrate at school or work, lose sleep because your mind will not settle, or find it difficult to enjoy things you normally would.

Mental-health organizations distinguish ordinary, temporary anxiety from anxiety disorders partly by looking at how persistent the symptoms are and how significantly they affect someone's daily functioning.

## How anxiety can show up

Anxiety can affect your **mind, body and behavior** at the same time.

### In your mind

You may experience:

- Constant worrying
- Racing thoughts
- Difficulty concentrating
- Feeling that something bad is about to happen
- Irritability
- Difficulty switching off your thoughts
- Finding it difficult to make decisions

### In your body

Physical symptoms can include:

- A faster or noticeable heartbeat
- Sweating
- Shaking or trembling
- Muscle tension
- Dizziness or light-headedness
- Headaches
- Breathlessness
- Nausea or stomach discomfort
- Changes in appetite
- Difficulty sleeping

Not everyone experiences the same symptoms, and having one or more of these symptoms does not by itself establish a diagnosis. Anxiety can also overlap with physical health conditions, so persistent or worrying physical symptoms should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional.

## Why does anxiety happen?

There is rarely one single explanation for anxiety.

It can develop or become stronger in response to circumstances happening in your life, experiences from the past, ongoing stress, health concerns, relationships, financial pressure, work or school demands, or social isolation.

Sometimes several factors interact.

For example, someone dealing with academic pressure may begin sleeping poorly. Poor sleep can make it harder to manage stress, which may increase worrying, which can make sleep even more difficult.

This can create a cycle that is difficult to break without support.

Research and mental-health guidance also recognize that social and environmental circumstances can influence anxiety. Experiences such as difficult life events, childhood adversity, isolation and ongoing social pressures can play a role.
