# Loneliness: what it is

You can be surrounded by people and still feel alone. You can have hundreds of followers and nobody you feel comfortable calling. You can sit in a crowded classroom, office, church, hostel or event and still feel disconnected.

Loneliness is not always about how many people are around you. Sometimes it is about whether you feel understood, connected and able to be yourself.

## What is loneliness?

Loneliness is the feeling that the connection you have with other people isn't meeting your emotional or social needs.

It is different from simply being alone. Some people genuinely enjoy spending time by themselves and don't feel lonely. Others can spend hours with friends or family and still experience a deep sense of isolation.

There is no single way loneliness feels.

## What can loneliness look like?

Loneliness may show up emotionally, physically or through changes in behavior. You might:

- Feel disconnected from people
- Feel like nobody understands you
- Want companionship but struggle to reach out
- Avoid social situations
- Spend more time alone than you would like
- Lose motivation to take care of yourself
- Find it difficult to sleep
- Feel anxious about interacting with others
- Compare your life with other people's lives
- Feel empty even when you're around people

Sometimes loneliness can also make social connection feel more difficult. You want to reach out, but you don't know what to say, so you stay quiet. The longer you stay disconnected, the harder reaching out may feel.

## Why do people become lonely?

There are many possible reasons. You might have moved to a new city, started university, changed jobs, lost someone important, ended a relationship, or become a caregiver. You might be dealing with social anxiety, or have stopped talking to people you were once close to. Or you may simply have reached a point in life where your relationships no longer feel as meaningful as they once did.

Sometimes there is no obvious explanation, and that's okay. You don't need a perfect explanation before your feelings deserve attention.

## Social media can make loneliness complicated

Being digitally connected doesn't necessarily mean feeling emotionally connected.

You might scroll through photographs of friends going out while you're sitting alone. You might see classmates celebrating achievements and wonder why your own life feels different. You might constantly interact online without having a conversation where you can honestly say: "I'm not doing okay."

Social media can create connection, but comparison can also make loneliness feel heavier. What people post is only part of their lives.

## How loneliness can affect you

Loneliness that persists can affect emotional wellbeing. It can be associated with stress, anxiety, low mood and depression.

It can also influence behavior. You may stop reaching out because you assume nobody wants to hear from you. That assumption can increase isolation, and isolation can then strengthen the original belief.

Breaking that cycle may require a small act of connection.
