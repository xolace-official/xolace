# Stress: what it is

- Deadlines.
- Bills.
- Exams.
- Relationships.
- Work.
- Family responsibilities.
- Uncertainty about the future.

Life can place pressure on us in countless ways, and sometimes our minds and bodies respond by saying:

*"This is becoming too much."*

That response is often what we call stress.

## What is stress?

Stress is a response that can occur when we feel under pressure, threatened, overwhelmed or unable to cope with what is happening around us.

It isn't always harmful.

A certain amount of stress can help you focus, prepare for an important event or respond quickly when something requires your attention.

The problem can arise when the pressure continues without enough opportunity to recover.

When stress becomes persistent, it can affect your physical health, emotions, thoughts and behavior.

## How do you know you're stressed?

Stress can appear in ways that aren't always obvious.

### Your body might respond

You may experience:

- Headaches
- Muscle tension
- Stomach discomfort
- Faster heartbeat
- Dizziness
- Tiredness
- Changes in sleep
- Changes in appetite

### Your thoughts might change

You may find yourself:

- Worrying constantly
- Struggling to concentrate
- Feeling overwhelmed
- Forgetting things
- Finding decisions difficult
- Thinking about problems repeatedly

### Your behavior might change

You may:

- Become more irritable
- Withdraw from people
- Avoid responsibilities
- Sleep much more or less
- Eat more or less than usual
- Rely on unhealthy habits to cope

Sometimes the first sign that you are stressed is not a thought at all.

It may be the way you suddenly start treating people, sleeping, eating or responding to everyday situations.

## What causes stress?

Almost anything that creates pressure can contribute to stress.

For one person, it may be academic demands.

For another, it may be financial uncertainty.

Someone else may be dealing with relationship difficulties, caregiving responsibilities, workplace pressure, illness or a major life change.

Stress can also build slowly.

One difficult situation may be manageable.

Several difficult situations happening at the same time can feel very different.

## The stress cycle

- Imagine having an important deadline.
- You worry about finishing.
- Because you're worried, you sleep poorly.
- Because you slept poorly, concentrating becomes harder.
- Because concentrating is harder, the work takes longer.
- Because the work takes longer, you become even more worried.
- And the cycle continues.

Recognizing these cycles can help you identify where you might be able to interrupt them.
