# Depression: what it is

Everyone has difficult days.

You might wake up feeling discouraged, lose interest in something you normally enjoy, or have a period when everything seems harder than usual. Those experiences are part of being human.

Depression is different.

Depression can affect the way a person feels, thinks, behaves and manages everyday life. It can influence sleep, appetite, concentration, relationships, school, work and the things that once brought enjoyment.

And sometimes, depression can develop without an obvious reason.

## What is depression?

Depression is a mental health condition involving persistent low mood or a significant loss of interest or pleasure in activities.

It is not simply a matter of being sad or deciding to "think positively."

A person experiencing depression may genuinely want to feel better but find that their thoughts, energy and emotions make ordinary activities feel difficult.

A depressive episode commonly involves symptoms occurring most of the day, nearly every day, for at least two weeks, although experiences can vary and only a qualified professional can make a diagnosis.

## What can depression feel like?

Depression doesn't look identical in everyone.

For some people, it may feel like sadness or emptiness. For others, it may feel more like numbness, irritability, exhaustion or disconnection.

You might notice:

- Losing interest in activities you previously enjoyed
- Feeling persistently sad, empty or hopeless
- Having very little energy
- Finding it difficult to concentrate
- Feeling worthless or excessively guilty
- Sleeping much more or much less than usual
- Experiencing changes in appetite
- Withdrawing from friends or family
- Struggling to complete normal responsibilities
- Feeling slowed down or unusually restless

Some people may also experience thoughts about death or suicide.

Having one of these experiences does not automatically mean someone has depression. What matters is the overall pattern, duration and effect on the person's life.

## Why does depression happen?

There is no single explanation that applies to everyone.

Depression can emerge from a combination of biological, psychological and social factors.

Difficult experiences such as bereavement, relationship problems, financial pressure, unemployment, academic difficulties, abuse, illness or prolonged stress can contribute. Family history and other health conditions may also influence someone's vulnerability.

Sometimes several pressures build up gradually until a person reaches a point where coping becomes increasingly difficult.

And sometimes someone becomes depressed without being able to identify a specific trigger.

That does not make their experience any less real.

## Depression can change the way you see yourself

One of the difficult things about depression is that it can affect more than your mood.

It can change the story you tell yourself.

A missed deadline can become:

*"I'm a failure."*

A difficult week can become:

*"Nothing is ever going to improve."*

A period of isolation can become:

*"Nobody really cares about me."*

Depression can make negative thoughts feel like facts.

Recognizing that these thoughts may be symptoms rather than objective conclusions can be an important step toward seeking support.

## Depression and everyday life

Depression can make ordinary responsibilities feel enormous.

Getting out of bed may require significant effort. Answering messages can feel exhausting. Going to class or work may become difficult. You may stop attending social events because you don't have the energy to explain how you feel.

This can create a cycle: withdrawing can increase isolation, isolation can deepen low mood, and low mood can make reconnecting feel even harder.

That is one reason early support can matter.
