# Make or break: the friends who carried me to first class

There's one question I get asked more than any other: "How did you finish so well? How did you graduate top of your class with first class honours?"

For a long time my honest answer was "I don't really know." It didn't make sense to me either, and here's why.

## Starting again, scared

SHS was not my best period, academically or otherwise. That story is for another day, but it was bad. I finished with very poor grades, close to failing. I was so disappointed in myself that I decided not to continue my education at all, and I spent the next two years away from school.

Eventually it was decided that I'd go back. Luckily, a school accepted me, and it offered computer networking on top of that. It felt like a chance to make up for the disappointment of my last results. But I'll be honest: I was scared out of my mind. I was sure I'd fail again.

I was so anxious that before school had even started, I was asking around about our first-semester courses. Anyone I heard had finished with a first class, I'd reach out to, whether I knew them or not. Most of them gave the same answers: read a lot, spend most of your time studying. It sounded like a huge amount of effort, because getting a first class at university was a big deal then and it still is. Those answers didn't help much. So in the end I just jumped in, scared.

## Settling for "second class is fine"

As the semester went on, I started connecting with students in the levels above me, the Level 200s and 300s. A few of them were strong both technically and academically, in the first class bracket. That combination is rare. Most people are one or the other. To me, being both looked like more than I could handle, so I made a choice: I'd focus on getting technical and do moderately well academically. A second class would be fine.

After the first semester exams, I could see that plan working. A second class wasn't a far-fetched dream anymore.

Then second semester changed everything.

## "Hey, can we join you?"

Two guys I knew were book smart, but not very technical at the time, came up to me out of nowhere and said, "Hey, can we join you? We want to build some practical skills." I said, "Yeah, cool, I can help with that."

I had no idea how much they would shape my growth and my grades. Those two guys are people you may already know as the co-founders of Xolace: Kobby and Kwame.

The group just worked from the start, because it was balanced. Kwame was outstanding academically. Kobby sat between the two, with a creative streak. I was the technical one. Each of us covered a gap the others had, so whatever we hit, someone in the group could take the lead.

Exams, which can be a constant source of stress throughout university, stopped being a nightmare. The first class I had written off as too much effort started to feel within reach, because I wasn't carrying the whole load alone. I was carrying my part.

So when people ask how I did it, I think I finally have an answer. It wasn't one big secret. It was the people around me.

## My take-home message

What I realised during those three years, and what I wish someone had told me before I started:

- **In your main friend group, don't be the only smart one, and definitely not the smartest at everything.** You can have any kind and any number of friends. But in your core circle, when the strengths are spread across people, everyone grows faster and nobody is overwhelmed.
- **Say yes when someone asks to learn from you.** I thought I was helping Kobby and Kwame. They ended up helping me just as much. Teaching what you know is one of the fastest ways to get access to what you don't know.
- **Generic advice won't carry you, but people will.** "Read a lot" isn't a plan. Find people a level or two ahead of you and ask what actually worked for them. Then find people beside you who are strong where you're weak.
- **Your past results don't decide your future ones.** I nearly failed SHS and spent two years out of school. If you're starting university scared that history will repeat itself, start anyway. Being scared isn't the same as not being ready.
- **Don't lower your goal just because it looks like too much effort alone.** I settled for "second class is fine" because I pictured doing everything myself. With the right people around you, the bigger goal might be closer than it looks.

Long story short: choose your people carefully. They'll either make you or break you.
