# The girl who prayed for a numb heart

I was only six years old.

Too young to understand bills. Too young to understand why my mum came home so tired. Too young to understand why fathers leave.

But old enough to miss him.

I remember that day like it was yesterday. My mum was away at work. I took the other phone in the house and called my dad.

"Daddy," I whispered. My whole little heart was in that word.

And on the other side, there was silence. Then a cold voice.

"What do you want? Why haven't you called all this while and decided to call today?"

That was all it took to break my world.

He went on to call my mum and warn her not to disturb him again. This broke my mum, and she came back home mad at me. I didn't cry that time. I held it in until I was alone, and then the tears came like a flood that would not stop. That night, I lay in my small bed and made a vow to God. A vow no six-year-old should ever have to make: that I would never call him again. And guess what? I'm turning 20 in a few days, and I have never called him since.

There came a time when I prayed, "God, please make my heart numb, so I will never feel this pain again."

But God did not make my heart numb. Because a numb heart cannot love. And I was made to love.

So I grew up feeling everything. Every night before I sleep, I still remember him. I close my eyes and imagine a perfect life. Not a life with toys and big houses. No. I imagine a life where my mum wouldn't have to work extra to pay our bills. Where she wouldn't have to be so tired. Where someone else would help her carry the weight.

My mum became my backbone. The strongest woman I have ever known. She paid the bills. She raised me and my sister alone. She did what he refused to do: she stayed.

And I watched her, and I learned what strength looked like. Strength was not never crying. Strength was crying, and then waking up the next morning and trying again.

But the pain was still there. It lived inside me, in the little girl who still lives inside me.

Then there came a time when I got tired of being angry. Tired of carrying the hurt. So I started praying a different prayer.

"God, give me the strength to forgive him, so my heart can be at peace."

And God answered. Not by making me forget him completely. I still remember him every night. I still imagine how life would have been if he had stuck around. But now, there is peace in the middle of the memory.

And then, one night, I sat down and wrote a letter. A letter I never intended to give him. A letter for me. For the little girl inside me.

This is what I wrote:

> *Dear Dad,*
>
> *I've given up hope that you'll be the father I need. I'm not angry anymore, but you should know you've disappointed the little girl that lives inside of me.*
>
> *The saddest thing of all is she would've forgiven you one hundred times over if you had asked her to. But me? I'll never forgive you for hurting her.*
>
> *She never asked for anything but love. You couldn't even provide the one thing that is supposed to be given so naturally. Do you know what that does to the psyche?*
>
> *I do. Because of you, I drown myself in self-help, therapy and gut-wrenching poetry.*

I put the pen down and cried. Not because I was broken. But because for the first time, I had finally said it. I had finally stood up for that six-year-old girl.

She would have forgiven him a hundred times. But the woman I am becoming will not let him hurt her one more time.

Now I am turning 20.

I am not the girl with the numb heart anymore. I am the girl who prayed for peace and found it. I am the girl who loves her mum fiercely. I am the girl who survived the phone call that should have broken her.

And my wish for 20 is simple.

To be that girl. The strong one. The happier one.

The girl who is finally at peace.
