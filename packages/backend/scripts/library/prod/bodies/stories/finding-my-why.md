# Finding my "why": studying, building, and the people who push you higher

When many of us arrive at university, we're still children in a lot of ways. The modern university isn't really ready for that, and honestly, we aren't ready for it either. Suddenly we have freedom, responsibilities, and pressures that nobody prepared us for, and the choices we make about who we spend time with can shape everything that follows.

For a long time, my way of handling it was to shut everything out. From Level 100 until I graduated with my HND in Computer Network Administration, I focused only on my academics. I kept away from relationships and anything outside my studies. Looking back, it wasn't that I didn't need people. I just didn't have an avenue to talk to anyone or build that kind of connection, so I kept everything to myself and kept going.

## The space I never had

Then I made a decision that changed everything: I chose the path of an entrepreneur. Alongside my co-founders, I started building Xolace, an app for the heavy, confusing feelings that don't have a name yet. So many students carry something every day that isn't "bad enough" for therapy but is too heavy to ignore, and they have nowhere to put it. Xolace gives them a private, anonymous place to say what's true, put words to what they feel, and see that someone else has felt it too.

I understood that gap because I had lived in it. I knew what it was like to have no one to talk to and nowhere to put what I was carrying. Building Xolace felt like creating the space I never had.

## Lecture notes on one side, onboarding screens on the other

Balancing school with the duties of a co-founder was tough. Some nights I would sit with my lecture notes open on one side, trying to prepare for a quiz or an exam, while the onboarding screens for Xolace waited on the other. The UI flow had to be finished, because the onboarding is the first thing a new user sees, and if it doesn't feel right, they might never come back. But the exam wasn't going to wait either. I'd switch between revising and designing, never feeling fully present in either, and wondering if I was doing both badly.

Building Xolace was exhausting and exciting at the same time. Knowing that a real gap existed, and that we were working to close it, gave me a feeling I won't get over anytime soon.

## The people who pushed me

And the irony is that building a space for connection is what finally connected me. After years of keeping people at a distance, working closely with my co-founders showed me what I had been missing. They pushed me into places I would never have gone on my own.

When we decided to launch *Behind Her Smile*, a TikTok mini-series, I had to step into the role of producer and director. I had never worked on a film project before, not even a small one. I was learning as I went, figuring out how to plan scenes, guide the people in front of the camera, and bring the story to life. It was uncomfortable, but it helped me grow in ways my studies alone never could have.

That's what surrounding yourself with people who challenge you does: it forces you to crack out of your current level and reach heights you didn't know were possible.

## Find your why

Looking back, I see university differently now. It isn't just a place to collect a certificate. It's a foundation, and the people you build alongside matter as much as the grades you earn.

If you're a student reading this, here's what I'd ask of you: find your "why." Why are you here? What do you want university to be the foundation for? Once you know, focus on it, work towards it, and surround yourself with people who will push you closer to it.

And if you're carrying something you can't put into words yet, you don't have to carry it alone.
