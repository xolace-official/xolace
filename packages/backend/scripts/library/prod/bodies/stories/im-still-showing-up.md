# I'm still showing up

There is a version of me that most people know.

She is usually smiling.

She is the one making jokes in the group chat, making people laugh in class, helping someone figure out an assignment, talking about software development, attending meetings, working on projects, showing up for communities, and somehow finding the energy to encourage everyone else.

She looks like she has things figured out.

Sometimes, I wish that version of me was the whole story.

But it isn't.

There is also a quieter version of me that people don't always get to see.

The one who goes home and sometimes has to deal with things that are difficult to explain. The one who has spent years carrying certain experiences from childhood that affected the way she sees herself. The one who sometimes struggles with her confidence because, somewhere along the way, she learned to question whether she was good enough.

And sometimes, home is not necessarily the place where you get to completely rest.

I think a lot of people understand what it feels like to have a difficult home environment and still have to wake up the next morning and behave like everything is normal.

You still have lectures.

Assignments still have deadlines.

People still need you.

Work still has to be done.

Messages still come in.

And somehow, you are expected to keep moving.

I remember one particular day.

Something happened at home that left me physically hurt and emotionally shaken. I had marks on my body, but more than that, I was carrying the kind of pain that doesn't show up in photographs.

And the next day, I had a video to shoot for the Xolace Behind Her Smile series.

So I went.

I showed up.

I smiled.

I did what I was supposed to do.

I stood in front of a camera and acted okay while I was still hurting, physically and mentally, from what had happened the day before.

And nobody looking at those videos would have known.

That is one of the strangest things about difficult seasons. You can be hurting deeply and still look completely fine to everyone else.

I remember thinking about who I would even call if I needed to go somewhere and just cry. Who I could tell, "This happened, and I don't know what to do with it."

And then I remembered that my mother isn't here anymore.

That is another kind of pain I am still learning how to carry.

I don't think I have figured out grief yet.

I still have moments when I wish I could call my mum. I still see people who have beautiful relationships with their fathers and feel something inside me ache. Not because I don't want that happiness for them, but because sometimes watching someone experience something you wish you had can remind you of what you don't.

There are things about losing a parent that you don't simply "get over."

You learn to live around the absence.

And sometimes, the absence is louder than usual.

That day, I had to put all of that somewhere inside me and continue with my life.

Because the world doesn't always pause when you're hurting.

For a long time, I have struggled with balancing everything.

School. Work. Career. Leadership. Communities. My personal life. And whatever is happening at home.

Sometimes I look at everything I have going on and wonder how I am supposed to keep up with all of it when I am already mentally exhausted.

There are days when I disappear.

I go quiet.

I stop posting.

I don't want to talk to anyone.

I don't want to explain myself.

Sometimes I just need to be alone with my thoughts until I can breathe properly again.

And I used to feel guilty about that.

Especially because people know me as someone who is always doing something.

So when I suddenly disappear, it can probably look like I am being distant or I have lost interest.

Sometimes, I am just tired.

Sometimes, I am trying to put myself back together quietly.

And then there is the frustrating part.

I know I have potential.

I know there is so much I can do.

Sometimes I look at myself and think, Rose, you haven't even scratched the surface yet.

There are so many things I want to learn. So many ideas I want to build. So many places I want to go. So many things I want to accomplish.

And sometimes I get frustrated because I know I am capable of more, but I also know that life doesn't always give you the perfect conditions to become the person you want to be.

Sometimes you're trying to build a future while simultaneously trying to survive the present.

And that can be exhausting.

But lately, I have been learning to look at myself differently.

Maybe I haven't done everything I thought I would by now.

Maybe I am not operating at my full potential.

Maybe there are days when I am slower than I want to be.

Maybe there are seasons where I have to step back, disappear for a little while, and take care of myself.

But I am still here.

And I think that counts for something.

Because despite everything, I am still showing up.

I am still studying.

I am still learning.

I am still building things.

I am still involved in communities.

I am still trying.

I am still dreaming.

I am still finding reasons to laugh.

I am still making other people laugh, even on days when I don't feel like laughing myself.

And maybe that is something worth acknowledging.

We talk a lot about resilience as if it always looks impressive.

Like waking up at 5 a.m., being productive, getting straight A's, building successful projects, attending every event, and constantly becoming a better version of yourself.

But sometimes resilience is much quieter.

Sometimes resilience is attending the lecture when you barely slept.

Sometimes it is submitting the assignment when your mind is somewhere else.

Sometimes it is opening your laptop and writing one line of code.

Sometimes it is replying to one message.

Sometimes it is getting out of bed.

Sometimes it is taking a break without feeling guilty.

Sometimes it is simply deciding that whatever happened yesterday will not be the reason you completely give up on tomorrow.

I am learning that I don't have to pretend that everything is okay just because I am still functioning.

I can be struggling and still be ambitious.

I can be tired and still have dreams.

I can have difficult days and still be proud of myself.

I can need help and still be strong.

And I can take my time.

If you are reading this and you are also carrying something quietly, I hope you know that you don't have to have everything figured out right now.

I hope you are gentle with yourself.

Your life is not behind because you are having a difficult season.

You are not lazy because you had to rest.

You are not weak because something affected you.

And you are not failing just because you haven't become everything you know you can be yet.

Sometimes, surviving the season you're in is part of becoming the person you want to be.

So, no.

I haven't done everything I want to do.

I probably haven't even done ten percent of what I believe I am capable of.

There are still parts of me I am trying to rebuild. There are still things I am learning to unlearn. There are still days when simply showing up feels like an achievement.

But I am still showing up.

And maybe that's enough for today.

One day, I will look back at this version of myself and realise that she was stronger than she thought.

Until then, I'll keep learning.

I'll keep building.

I'll keep dreaming.

I'll keep showing up.

Even when it's hard.

Especially when it's hard.
