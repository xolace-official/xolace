# Anxiety: what helps

## Anxiety and everyday life

Anxiety is not simply something that happens inside your head.

When it becomes persistent, it can influence how you study, work, communicate, sleep and interact with other people.

You might:

- Put off important tasks because they feel overwhelming.
- Avoid places or situations that make you uncomfortable.
- Repeatedly seek reassurance from other people.
- Find it difficult to focus on conversations.
- Spend large amounts of time worrying about future situations.
- Withdraw from friends or activities you previously enjoyed.

Avoidance can sometimes provide temporary relief, but continually avoiding situations that trigger anxiety can also reinforce the fear surrounding them. Psychological treatments such as cognitive behavioral therapy often address this pattern.

## What can you do when anxiety feels overwhelming?

There is no single technique that works for everyone. The goal is not necessarily to eliminate every anxious feeling, but to develop healthier ways of responding to it.

### 1. Give your thoughts some space

When your mind is running through every possible negative outcome, pause and identify what is actually happening right now.

Ask yourself:

**"What do I know for certain, and what am I imagining might happen?"**

Separating facts from predictions can sometimes make an overwhelming situation feel more manageable.

### 2. Talk to someone

You do not have to explain everything perfectly.

Talking with a trusted friend, family member, counsellor or mental-health professional can give you an opportunity to express what you have been carrying alone.

Sometimes being heard is an important first step.

### 3. Look after your body

Physical wellbeing and emotional wellbeing are closely connected.

Regular movement, adequate sleep, nutritious meals and reducing excessive caffeine or other substances that can worsen anxiety may help you manage symptoms.

### 4. Try controlled breathing

When anxiety rises, your body can move into a heightened state of alertness.

Slow, controlled breathing can be one simple technique for helping you pause and settle your body.

Try breathing slowly and comfortably rather than forcing deep breaths. Focus your attention on the sensation of breathing and allow your body time to relax.

### 5. Take things one step at a time

Anxiety can make every problem feel urgent.

Instead of trying to solve everything at once, identify the next manageable action.

Sometimes the next step is simply sending an email, taking a short walk, completing one assignment, making a phone call, or asking someone for help.

Small steps still count.

## When should you seek professional help?

Consider speaking with a qualified mental-health or healthcare professional if anxiety:

- Is becoming difficult to control
- Continues for a long period
- Interferes with school, work or relationships
- Prevents you from doing things you want or need to do
- Is affecting your sleep significantly
- Causes frequent panic or intense distress
- Is leading you to rely on alcohol or other substances to cope

You do not have to wait until things become unbearable before asking for help.

Anxiety disorders are treatable, and professional support can include talking therapies such as cognitive behavioral therapy and, in some circumstances, medication prescribed by an appropriate healthcare professional.

## Anxiety doesn't have to define your life

Experiencing anxiety does not make you weak, incapable or alone.

Many people experience periods when worry becomes difficult to manage. What matters is recognizing when those feelings are beginning to control your choices or interfere with your everyday life.

You can start by paying attention to what you are experiencing, talking to someone you trust, learning strategies that help you cope, and seeking professional support when you need it.

You don't have to figure everything out at once.
